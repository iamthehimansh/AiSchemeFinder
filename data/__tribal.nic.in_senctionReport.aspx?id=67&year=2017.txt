The programmes and schemes of the Ministry are intended to support and supplement other Central Ministries, State Governments and partly of voluntary organizations, and to fill critical gaps in institutions and programmes taking into account the situation of STs through financial assistance. These schemes comprising economic, educational and social development through institution building are administered by the Ministry and implemented mainly through the State Governments/ Union Territory Administrations. The Ministry also supplements the efforts of other Ministries by way of various developmental interventions in critical sectors through specially tailored schemes. About Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) Ministry of Tribal Affairs is fully commited to the smooth and effective implementation of the Direct Benefits Transfer (DBT). DBT aims to bring transparency, eliminate misuse of funds and enable enhanced monitoring of the Schemes implemented by the Government of India. In DBT, benefit or subsidy will be directly transferred to the bank accounts of the beneficiaries. Public Finance Management System (PFMS) acts as the common Platform for DBT. All State Governments/ UTs shall upload data regarding DBT Transactions on the portal for every month by the 4th day of the next month. Sh. Jual Oram Hon'ble Minister Sh. Individuals with SCD have abnormal haemoglobin which distorts the shape off red blood cells, making them crescent moon or sickle shaped. These distorted red blood cells get stuck in the small blood vessels (capillaries) in the body, blocking the blood flow. Transmission Being a genetic disorder, SCD is inherited by a child from his/her parents. Like most genes, haemoglobin genes are inherited in two sets, one from each parent. An individual in whom both the haemoglobin genes are abnormal suffers from Sickle Cell Disease. An individual in whom only one of the two genes is abnormal is said to be having Sickle Cell Trait. While individuals with Sickle Cell Trait do not have symptoms, they can transmit the gene to their child. If one parent has Sickle Cell Disease and the other is Normal, all of the children will have Sickle Cell Trait. If one parent has Sickle Cell Disease and the other has Sickle Cell Trait, there is a 50% chance of each child having either sickle cell disease or sickle cell trait. When both parents have Sickle Cell Trait, each of their children has a 25% chance of having sickle cell disease. Symptoms Some of the common signs and symptoms of SCD include Anemia (low haemoglobin), pain (known as sickle cell crisis), swelling of limbs, frequent infections, delayed growth, vision problems etc. Sickle Cell Disease in Tribal Groups SCD is widespread in many tribal groups of India especially among the Dravidian and pre-Dravidian tribes inhabiting malaria endemic regions. About 1 in 86 births among Scheduled Tribe (ST) population have SCD, the prevalence being higher in Central, Western and Southern India. The objective of the Tribal Sub-Plan (TSP) is to bridge the gap between Scheduled Tribe (ST) population and others by accelerating the development of STs by securing them: (i) Human resource development by enhancing access to education and health services, (ii) Enhanced quality of life by providing basic amenities in tribal areas/localities including housing; (iii) Substantial reduction in poverty and unemployment, creation of productive assets and income generating opportunities (iv) Enhanced capacity to avail opportunities, gain rights and entitlements and improved facilities at par with other areas, and (v) Protection against exploitation and oppression. As per the last census, the total tribal population in the country stands at 10,45,45,716 which comes to 8.6 percentage of the overall population of the country. Presently Tribal Sub Plan covers 22 States and 4 UTs that have notified tribes (Scheduled Tribes) (as per census 2011), and these tribes are in a minority in the State/UT population. The portal reflects Tribal Sub Plan allocations from State/UT Budgets. URL: https://scdiec.tribal.gov.in/SCD MISSION Home Dashboard Resources Awareness Module (English) Awareness Module (Hindi) Counseling Module (English) Counseling Module (Hindi) Standee (Hindi) Standee (English) Standee (Awareness) 1 Standee (Awareness) 2 Standee (Awareness) 3 Standee (Awareness) 4 Standee (Acheivements) Standee (Hindi) Blank Standee (English) Blank Backdrop (Hindi) Backdrop (English) Demo Video Convert JPEG to JPG Knowledge Repository Gallery Photo Gallery Video Gallery Awareness Videos Login Signup Contact Us Previous Next About Sickle Cell Disease Sickle Cell Disease (SCD) is a genetic condition that affects haemoglobin (responsible for carrying oxygen in the body) in red blood cells. Individuals with SCD have abnormal haemoglobin which distorts the shape off red blood cells, making them crescent moon or sickle shaped. These distorted red blood cells get stuck in the small blood vessels (capillaries) in the body, blocking the blood flow. Transmission Being a genetic disorder, SCD is inherited by a child from his/her parents. Like most genes, haemoglobin genes are inherited in two sets, one from each parent. An individual in whom both the haemoglobin genes are abnormal suffers from Sickle Cell Disease. An individual in whom only one of the two genes is abnormal is said to be having Sickle Cell Trait. While individuals with Sickle Cell Trait do not have symptoms, they can transmit the gene to their child. If one parent has Sickle Cell Disease and the other is Normal, all of the children will have Sickle Cell Trait. If one parent has Sickle Cell Disease and the other has Sickle Cell Trait, there is a 50% chance of each child having either sickle cell disease or sickle cell trait. When both parents have Sickle Cell Trait, each of their children has a 25% chance of having sickle cell disease. Symptoms Some of the common signs and symptoms of SCD include Anemia (low haemoglobin), pain (known as sickle cell crisis), swelling of limbs, frequent infections, delayed growth, vision problems etc. Sickle Cell Disease in Tribal Groups SCD is widespread in many tribal groups of India especially among the Dravidian and pre-Dravidian tribes inhabiting malaria endemic regions. About 1 in 86 births among Scheduled Tribe (ST) population have SCD, the prevalence being higher in Central, Western and Southern India. The objective of the Tribal Sub-Plan (TSP) is to bridge the gap between Scheduled Tribe (ST) population and others by accelerating the development of STs by securing them: (i) Human resource development by enhancing access to education and health services, (ii) Enhanced quality of life by providing basic amenities in tribal areas/localities including housing; (iii) Substantial reduction in poverty and unemployment, creation of productive assets and income generating opportunities (iv) Enhanced capacity to avail opportunities, gain rights and entitlements and improved facilities at par with other areas, and (v) Protection against exploitation and oppression. The programmes and schemes of the Ministry are intended to support and supplement other Central Ministries, State Governments and partly of voluntary organizations, and to fill critical gaps in institutions and programmes taking into account the situation of STs through financial assistance. Individuals with SCD have abnormal haemoglobin which distorts the shape off red blood cells, making them crescent moon or sickle shaped. These distorted red blood cells get stuck in the small blood vessels (capillaries) in the body, blocking the blood flow. Transmission Being a genetic disorder, SCD is inherited by a child from his/her parents. Like most genes, haemoglobin genes are inherited in two sets, one from each parent. An individual in whom both the haemoglobin genes are abnormal suffers from Sickle Cell Disease. An individual in whom only one of the two genes is abnormal is said to be having Sickle Cell Trait. While individuals with Sickle Cell Trait do not have symptoms, they can transmit the gene to their child. If one parent has Sickle Cell Disease and the other is Normal, all of the children will have Sickle Cell Trait. If one parent has Sickle Cell Disease and the other has Sickle Cell Trait, there is a 50% chance of each child having either sickle cell disease or sickle cell trait. When both parents have Sickle Cell Trait, each of their children has a 25% chance of having sickle cell disease. Symptoms Some of the common signs and symptoms of SCD include Anemia (low haemoglobin), pain (known as sickle cell crisis), swelling of limbs, frequent infections, delayed growth, vision problems etc. Sickle Cell Disease in Tribal Groups SCD is widespread in many tribal groups of India especially among the Dravidian and pre-Dravidian tribes inhabiting malaria endemic regions. About 1 in 86 births among Scheduled Tribe (ST) population have SCD, the prevalence being higher in Central, Western and Southern India. Source : Inputs received from States/ UTs from time to time ( * )  :  TSP Expenditure based on Quarterly Data About State Tribal Sub Plan : Monitoring of Tribal Sub-Plan, based on the framework and mechanism designed by NITI Aayog, is one of the items allocated to the Ministry of Tribal Affairs under the Allocation of Business Rules. The objective of the Tribal Sub-Plan (TSP) is to bridge the gap between Scheduled Tribe (ST) population and others by accelerating the development of STs by securing them: (i) Human resource development by enhancing access to education and health services, (ii) Enhanced quality of life by providing basic amenities in tribal areas/localities including housing; (iii) Substantial reduction in poverty and unemployment, creation of productive assets and income generating opportunities (iv) Enhanced capacity to avail opportunities, gain rights and entitlements and improved facilities at par with other areas, and (v) Protection against exploitation and oppression. As per the last census, the total tribal population in the country stands at 10,45,45,716 which comes to 8.6 percentage of the overall population of the country. Presently Tribal Sub Plan covers 22 States and 4 UTs that have notified tribes (Scheduled Tribes) (as per census 2011), and these tribes are in a minority in the State/UT population. The portal reflects Tribal Sub Plan allocations from State/UT Budgets. This significant date marks the birth anniversary of the revered tribal freedom fighter, Dharti Aba Bhagwan Birsa Munda , and is dedicated to honoring the bravery, sacrifices, and invaluable contributions of tribal freedom fighters who played a pivotal role in India’s struggle for independence. This annual observance ensures that future generations recognize and cherish this crucial part of our nation's history. Recognizing Tribal Contributions Hon’ble Prime Minister Shri Narendra Modi has consistently emphasized the role of tribal communities in shaping India's freedom narrative. Through various platforms, including his Mann Ki Baat addresses, he has highlighted the contributions of tribal heroes who resisted colonial exploitation. In his Independence Day speech from the Red Fort on August 15, 2016, the Prime Minister announced the establishment of tribal freedom fighter museums across the country. Today, eleven such museums are sanctioned, including the Bhagwan Birsa Munda Freedom Fighter Museum in Ranchi, inaugurated in 2021 as a tribute to this great  tribal leader's legacy. Historic Initiatives and Milestones In a historic moment during Janjatiya Gaurav Divas 2023, Prime Minister Modi became the first Prime Minister to visit the birthplace of Bhagwan Birsa Munda. On this sacred ground, he launched two landmark initiatives: 1.	Pradhan Mantri Janjati Adivasi Nyay Maha Abhiyan (PM JANMAN): A comprehensive campaign focused on ensuring justice, rights, and opportunities for tribal communities. 2.	Viksit Bharat Abhiyan: An initiative aimed at driving the development and empowerment of India's tribal populations, aligning with the vision of a progressive India. In a further demonstration of the government's commitment to tribal welfare, the Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Utkarsh Gram Abhiyan was recently introduced. This program targets the holistic development of over 63,000 tribal-dominated villages, focusing on enhancing quality of life, preserving cultural heritage, and promoting sustainable growth. Commemorating the 150th Birth Anniversary of Bhagwan Birsa Munda As we mark the 150th birth anniversary year of Bhagwan Birsa Munda, Janjatiya Gaurav Divas 2024 will be celebrated with a nationwide spirit of Jan Bhagidari (people's participation). A whole-of-government approach will be adopted, featuring a series of events, exhibitions, cultural performances, and educational initiatives that highlight the legacy of tribal freedom fighters and the rich traditions of tribal communities across India. Join us in celebrating the Janjatiya Gaurav Divas 2024 as we honor the indomitable spirit, resilience, and pride of India's tribal heroes. Let us come together to pay tribute to their sacrifices, preserve their cultural heritage, and ensure their stories continue to inspire future generations. URL: https://adiprasaran.tribal.gov.in/pm-janmanPM- JANMAN 2.0 & DHARTI AABA JANJATIYA GRAM UTKARSH ABHIYAN GOVERNMENT OF INDIA | MINISTRY OF TRIBAL AFFAIRS Skip to Main Home PM-JANMAN 1.0 Resources PM-JUGA Guidelines PM-JANMAN Guidelines IEC Material IEC Material - PM-JUGA Videos Photos Convert JPEG to JPG SOP News PM-JANMAN GLIMPSES Login Signup Contact Us Previous Next About PM-JANMAN The Union Cabinet chaired by the Prime Minister, Shri Narendra Modi has approved Pradhan Mantri Janjati Adivasi Nyaya Maha Abhiyan (PM JANMAN) with total outlay of Rs.24,104 crore (Central Share:Rs.15,336 crore and State Share: Rs.8,768 crore) to focus on 11 critical interventions through 9 line Ministries.  The Prime Minister announced the Abhiyan on Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas from Khunti. As announced in the Budget Speech 2023-24, “to improve socio-economic conditions of the Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs), Pradhan Mantri PVTG of Development Mission will be launched.  This will saturate PVTG households and habitations with basic facilities such as safe housing, clean drinking water and sanitation, improved access to education, health and nutrition, road and telecom connectivity, and sustainable livelihood opportunities.  An amount of Rs.15,000 crore will be made available to implement the Mission in the next three years under the Development Action Plan for the Scheduled Tribes (DAPST).” India has ST population of 10.45 crore as per 2011 census, out of which 75 communities located in 18 States and the Union Territory of Andaman and Nicobar Islands have been categorized as Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTGs).  These PVTGs continue to face vulnerability in social, economic and educational fields. With an aim of reaching out to 22000 Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups (PVTG) majority tribal habitations and PVTG families in 200 districts across the country, Union Ministry of Tribal Affairs has initiated an Information, Education, and Communication (IEC) campaign for the Pradhan Mantri Janjati Adivasi Nyaya Maha Abhiyan (PM-JANMAN), from 25th December 2023 onwards A part of the campaign will also comprise of Beneficiary Saturation Camps and Health Camps. These camps will focus on providing immediate benefits under schemes for individual/household benefits and health related issues in PVTG habitations.   Awareness material like pamphlets, video, wall paintings, Jingles, Thematic Wall Paintings, Cultural Programs, are expected to be utilized in local and tribal languages. Officers at District level have been assigned for each district to supervise the campaign, while the State level officers will coordinate with different line departments of State governments to ensure the success of the campaign and Mission. Further, the Tribal Research Institutes in various States will assist to plan and execute these activities at the District, Block, and Tribal Habitation levels. The hashtags #EmpoweringTribalsTransformingIndia and #PMJANMAN will be strategically employed across all social media handles/posts, amplifying the campaign's impact and fostering a broader online dialogue.
